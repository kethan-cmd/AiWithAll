# Final: Top 3

3 rounds run (loop max). Top score rose each round: 7.14 -> 7.50 -> 7.75. 35 subagent calls. Scores are round-3 scores (Judge A skeptic + Judge B caregiver, weighted avg, 1-10). Per-idea detail in `round-1.md` to `round-3.md`.

Caveat: all three finalists descend from one root idea (R1-2, Practice the Ask): an AI role-play coach. The loop converged on a single mechanism, not three different ones.

## 1. Practice the Moment (R3-6): 7.75 (A 7.50 / B 8.00)

**Pitch.** Caregivers of a parent with Alzheimer's face the same daily flashpoints: "this isn't my food," "I want to go home," refusing a shower. In our app, the AI plays the parent, in the parent's language, so the caregiver can practice calm replies before the real moment. The AI coaches with commonly recommended techniques (validate, redirect) as general guidance, not medical advice, then saves the phrases that worked. Teen respite volunteers can practice before a visit, which adds service.

**Strongest evidence.**
- Our survey: need for practical, task-level guidance; 68% would join a support group if it were in their native language (aggregate, n=53).
- Nearly 13M Americans provide unpaid dementia care ([Alzheimer's Association Facts and Figures, verified](https://www.alz.org/alzheimers-dementia/facts-figures)).
- Skills training reduced caregiver burden and stress in a simulation study ([PMC11376966, verified](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11376966/)). Existing simulators are academic VR or in-person; no consumer AI parent role-play app found (brief search only).

**Biggest risk.** Theme fit is weak: the volunteer part is a bolt-on, so a judge may ask "where is the Day of Service?" Also: safety and taste. An AI imitating a person with dementia needs careful framing, and coaching must not read as medical advice.

**2-minute demo.**
1. 0:00 Hook: headline stat from our survey, then one caregiver's 6pm dinner standoff.
2. 0:20 Pick language and scenario "Refuses to eat".
3. 0:35 Live chat: the AI parent says "this isn't my food"; the caregiver tries arguing; the coach flags it.
4. 1:00 Retry with validate-and-redirect; the AI parent softens.
5. 1:20 Feedback card: what worked, plus a "not medical advice" line. Phrase saved to the phrase bank.
6. 1:35 A teen volunteer practices the same scenario before a respite visit; hours logged.
7. 1:50 Close: "practice the hard moment before it happens."

## 2. Bridge Volunteer Trainer (R2-2): 7.08 (A 7.28 / B 6.89)

**Pitch.** Many Asian American caregivers never ask for help because seeking it can feel like bringing shame on the family. Our app trains bilingual teen volunteers to reach them: the AI plays a hesitant caregiver ("we don't air family business"), and coaches the volunteer on respectful, shame-free ways to invite them to a native-language support group. Volunteers log real outreach conversations and referrals, so one trained teen can reach many families.

**Strongest evidence.**
- Our survey: majority agree seeking help can be seen as weakness; 68% would join a group in their native language; 61% want a culturally similar facilitator.
- Stigma lowers help-seeking among Asian American caregivers ([PubMed 36935622, verified](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36935622/)).
- Filial piety and stigma underlie underuse of formal dementia support ([Gerontologist 2024, verified](https://academic.oup.com/gerontologist/article/64/2/gnad086/7216957)).

**Biggest risk.** Impact is indirect: no caregiver benefits on screen, and referral outcomes are self-logged. Also, sending teens to approach strangers needs a safety story (school-verified, group settings only).

**2-minute demo.**
1. 0:00 Hook: survey stat on stigma; Day of Service framing.
2. 0:20 A volunteer picks "temple courtyard" and the language.
3. 0:35 The AI caregiver pushes back ("people will talk"); the volunteer's first invite is too pushy.
4. 1:05 The coach scores it and suggests shame-free wording; the volunteer retries and the AI caregiver opens up.
5. 1:30 Scored feedback card.
6. 1:40 The volunteer logs a real conversation and referral; the dashboard shows outreach count.
7. 1:55 Close: "one trained volunteer, many families."

## 3. Practice the Ask (R1-2): 6.89 (A 7.06 / B 6.72)

**Pitch.** Caregivers who fear judgment often never ask relatives for help. Our app is a private rehearsal room: the caregiver picks a relative, a language and a tone, and the AI plays that relative, including pushback like "we handle family matters ourselves." It then coaches wording that frames help as shared family duty, and saves a ready-to-send script.

**Strongest evidence.**
- Our survey: fear of family judgment and "help can be seen as weakness" (majority agree); shame concerns.
- Qualitative study: 91% of Asian American dementia caregivers mentioned stigma, linked to lower help-seeking ([PMC13053934, unverified: page not opened by researcher](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13053934/)).
- Generic AI role-play apps exist ([Vocal Image, verified](https://www.vocalimage.app/en/articles/37-practice-difficult-conversations-ai-apps/)); none found for caregivers or shame-to-shared-duty framing.

**Biggest risk.** Solo tool with no service element (theme fit 4/10 from both judges), and it doesn't reduce tomorrow's workload.

**2-minute demo.**
1. 0:00 Hook: survey stat on fear of judgment.
2. 0:15 Pick "older brother", language, and a gentle tone.
3. 0:30 The caregiver types an ask; the AI brother pushes back.
4. 1:00 The coach reframes as shared family duty; the caregiver retries and the brother softens.
5. 1:30 Feedback card.
6. 1:40 Saved script, one tap to copy to a message.
7. 1:55 Close.

## Verdict

**Yes, the winner beats Tag In on the rubric, with caveats.** Practice the Moment scored 7.75. Tag In scored 6.20 in round 1, where Practice the Ask (its parent) scored 7.14 in the same judging call, so the comparison is like-for-like. Later rounds re-scored survivors in new pools, and carried-over ideas drifted slightly down (Practice the Ask 7.14 -> 6.89), so treat the 7.75 vs 6.20 gap as directional, not exact. Tag In lost mainly on feasibility (3-5/10: check-in, trend, load chart, AI task splitting, family board, volunteer board, and relief score is too much for 7 hours), and its planned "your stress is higher than X% of caregivers" cannot be supported because our survey did not measure stress. Tag In still wins on theme fit (9/10, the family-plus-volunteer loop is a real Day of Service story) and on the emotional pitch. Practice the Moment and Practice the Ask are the easiest builds and give the most gripping live demo, but they are weaker on service. If theme fit worries you, Bridge Volunteer Trainer is the safer pick: it keeps the same buildable core, puts volunteers at the center, and uses our survey findings most directly. Note that judging was AI-simulated; a real caregiver's reaction (your pre-comp-day task) should override these numbers. Many evidence links were marked unverified by the researchers; open the ones you plan to cite before the pitch.
